Financial Aid and Scholarships

Types of Financial Aid
The university offers several forms of financial aid, including need-based grants, merit-based scholarships, federal work-study, and student loans. Aid eligibility is determined primarily through the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA), which should be submitted as early as possible after October 1 for the following academic year.

Merit Scholarships
Merit scholarships are awarded automatically to admitted students based on academic achievement, including GPA and standardized test scores; no separate application is required. Award amounts range from $2,000 to full tuition and are renewable each year as long as the student maintains a cumulative GPA of 3.0 or higher.

Work-Study Program
The Federal Work-Study program provides part-time employment opportunities on campus for students with demonstrated financial need. Students typically work between 10 and 15 hours per week, and earnings are paid directly to the student rather than applied to the tuition bill.

Satisfactory Academic Progress
To remain eligible for financial aid, students must maintain Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP), which includes a minimum cumulative GPA of 2.0, completion of at least 67% of attempted credit hours, and progression toward degree completion within 150% of the published length of the program.

Refunds and Withdrawals
Students who withdraw from all courses during a semester may be subject to a return of Title IV funds calculation, which determines how much federal aid must be returned based on the percentage of the semester completed. Institutional refunds for tuition follow a separate schedule published each semester by the Bursar's Office.

How to Appeal a Financial Aid Decision
Students who lose aid eligibility due to SAP violations may submit a written appeal to the Financial Aid Office, along with supporting documentation of extenuating circumstances such as illness or family emergency. Appeals are reviewed by committee, typically within 10 business days.
